NATURAL IGNITION · PREPAREDNESS

Natural wildfires

Natural events can ignite wildfires. Knowing how weather and vegetation affect fire behavior can help you prepare, but official alerts should guide decisions.

Back to the Safety Guide

HOW FIRES BEGIN

Lightning is a common natural cause

Lightning can heat and ignite vegetation. Some strikes start fires immediately, while others may smolder and become visible later.

In some places, volcanic activity can also ignite fires. The natural ignition itself cannot be prevented, so readiness and timely warnings matter.

Weather can increase fire danger

Hot, dry, and windy weather can dry vegetation and help an existing fire spread more quickly. Grasses, brush, and trees provide fuel.

Important: Weather and dry vegetation can increase risk and spread, but they are not usually the ignition source by themselves.

BE READY

What to do during high fire danger

Monitor official alerts

Sign up for local emergency notifications and monitor local authorities for fire and evacuation updates.

Know how you will leave

Plan routes and a meeting place. Keep essential items ready and plan for children, older adults, people with disabilities, and pets.

Leave when directed

Follow evacuation orders promptly. Do not wait to confirm a fire's cause or approach a fire to investigate.

GETTING HELP

Institutional response & who to call

Natural ignitions cannot be prevented, but agencies monitor fire conditions and coordinate warnings and response. Local services vary by location.

How institutions respond

  • Fire and rescue services assess reported fires, coordinate suppression and rescue, and may issue immediate safety instructions.
  • Emergency management and local authorities coordinate warnings, evacuation routes, shelters, and community updates.
  • Forestry or land-management agencies monitor fires and conditions on public or protected land and may announce closures.
  • Public health and air-quality agencies share guidance about smoke exposure and health precautions.

Who to call

  • Smoke, flames, or immediate danger: move to safety and call your local emergency number.
  • Evacuation or shelter information: check official local-authority or emergency-management channels.
  • Smoke or health concerns: follow local public-health and air-quality advice; contact a healthcare provider for medical concerns.

Emergency numbers differ by country and region. Use the number recognized where you are, follow official evacuation instructions, and never approach a fire to investigate.
