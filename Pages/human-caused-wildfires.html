HUMAN ACTIONS · PREVENTION

Wildfires caused by humans

Many wildfires begin with an avoidable human ignition. Learn the common causes and simple ways to lower the risk.

Explore common causes Back to the Safety Guide

HOW PEOPLE CAN START FIRES

Common human causes

The National Interagency Fire Center (NIFC) reports that, in the United States, human-caused fires made up 88% of the 10-year average of all wildfires as of 2023. This is a U.S. statistic, not a global estimate.

Campfires and outdoor activities

Campfires and other recreational fires can escape when left unattended or not fully extinguished. Follow local fire restrictions and make sure fires are completely out before leaving.

Debris and open burning

Burn piles, burn barrels, and other open burns may escape when embers carry into nearby dry vegetation. Check local rules and never leave a permitted burn unattended.

Smoking materials

Carelessly discarded cigarettes or other smoking materials can ignite dry grass and leaves. Use a proper container and never throw them from a vehicle.

Equipment, vehicles, and other sparks

Sparks from equipment or vehicles can ignite dry vegetation. Use and maintain equipment carefully, and avoid spark-producing work when local conditions make it unsafe.

SMALL ACTIONS MAKE A DIFFERENCE

Help prevent human-caused wildfires

Conditions and burn rules vary by location. Follow current guidance from your local fire and emergency authorities.

Check before burning

Check for local restrictions, permits, and weather conditions before any outdoor burning. Postpone when authorities advise against it.

Put fires out completely

Stay with permitted fires and extinguish them completely. Do not leave until all embers and heat are out.

Use care with equipment

Keep vehicles and equipment in good working order, and avoid activities that could create sparks near dry grass or brush.

References and trusted sources

The causes and U.S. statistic on this page are based on the National Interagency Fire Center's wildfire investigation information. Prevention guidance should always be adapted to local rules and current conditions.
