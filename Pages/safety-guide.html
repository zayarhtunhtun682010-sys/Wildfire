LEARN THE BASICS

Understanding Disasters

Disasters are events that can overwhelm local systems and interrupt daily life, especially when people do not have clear information or a plan.

What they are

A disaster may be sudden, such as a wildfire or earthquake, or it may develop over time, like severe smoke or drought conditions. In either case, the event disrupts safety, transport, communication, and basic services.

Why they become dangerous

Risk increases when people are unprepared, when conditions change quickly, or when communities lack warnings, clear routes, and support systems. Even a manageable incident can become serious when multiple needs arise at once.

How communities respond

Prepared communities rely on local alerts, trained responders, evacuation plans, shelters, and neighbor-to-neighbor support. Early action can reduce harm and make recovery more effective.

WHEN A WILDFIRE IS NEARBY

Act early. Follow local instructions.

Conditions can change quickly. Use official local alerts and emergency services for decisions about your area.

01

If told to evacuate

Evacuate immediately, by going the recommended route. Take members of your family with you, plus family pets. Medications and go-bag also. Do not stop for anything.

02

If you see danger

Move to a place that is clear of flames and smoke. When it is safe to do so call your local emergency number. Give people accurate information (not rumours)

03

If you need help

Contact emergency services or local authorities and state where you are and what support you need. Follow their instructions and take shelter at an official shelter if advised to do so.

PROTECT YOUR HEALTH

When smoke affects your area

Wildfire smoke can affect anyone, especially children, older adults, pregnant people, and people with heart or lung conditions.

Reduce exposure

Check local air-quality guidance. When advised, stay indoors, keep windows and doors closed, and use cleaner indoor air if available.

Get reliable updates

Monitor local health and emergency alerts. Follow your care plan and contact a health professional if symptoms worsen.

An N95 respirator may reduce exposure to particles when properly fitted, but it does not protect against all gases or make unsafe air safe.

RESPONSE AND SUPPORT

Institutional Response & Who to Call

Wildfire response is organized locally, and agency names and emergency numbers differ across Asia. Follow official instructions for the country and area where you are.

How institutions respond

  • Fire and rescue services respond to reported fires, protect people, and coordinate firefighting and rescue.
  • Local authorities and emergency management issue local warnings, evacuation directions, and shelter information.
  • Forestry and land-management agencies monitor fires and may coordinate response on forests or protected lands.
  • Environmental and public-health agencies share air-quality updates and advice for reducing smoke exposure.

Who to call

  • Fire, smoke, or immediate danger: move to safety and call the emergency number used in your location.
  • To report a wildfire: contact local fire and rescue services or the responsible forestry authority.
  • Evacuation or shelter updates: check local government and emergency-management channels.
  • Smoke or air-quality concerns: follow local environmental and public-health guidance.

Do not approach a fire to investigate. Emergency numbers and agency responsibilities vary by country; use official local information.
