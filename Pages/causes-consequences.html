UNDERSTAND THE RISKS

Wildfire causes and consequences

Wildfires can start naturally or through human activity. Their impacts depend on where they burn, available fuel, weather, and how prepared communities are.

Back to the Safety Guide

CAUSES AND FIRE BEHAVIOR

A fire needs an ignition source and fuel

Understanding the difference between ignition and fire-spreading conditions helps explain why risk can change quickly.

Ignition

Lightning and human activities such as unattended campfires or sparks from equipment can start wildfires. Prevention can reduce some human-caused ignitions.

Fuel

Dry grasses, brush, trees, and other vegetation can feed a fire. Local landscapes and the condition of vegetation influence how fire moves.

Weather

Heat, low humidity, drought, and wind can dry fuels or contribute to rapid fire spread. These conditions do not necessarily start a fire on their own.

POSSIBLE IMPACTS

Wildfires affect more than the burn area

  • Flames and embers can threaten people, homes, roads, and essential services.
  • Smoke can travel far and worsen air quality, affecting breathing and heart health.
  • Evacuations can disrupt daily life, separate households, and displace people and animals.
  • Fire can damage habitats and soil; heavy rain afterward may increase erosion or runoff in some places.

PREPARE AND PROTECT

Steps households can take

  • Reduce avoidable ignition risks and follow local fire restrictions.
  • Prepare a go-bag, choose meeting places, and plan for transport and pets.
  • Know more than one evacuation route and sign up for local emergency alerts.
  • Follow official evacuation and air-quality advice; return only when authorities say it is safe.
