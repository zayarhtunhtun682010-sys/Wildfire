ABOUT THE ISSUE

What Is a Wildfire?

A wildfire is an uncontrolled fire that spreads through forests, grasslands, or other natural areas. It can happen naturally or be caused by human activities.

Wildfires can destroy habitats, damage communities, pollute the air, and threaten people's safety. Understanding their causes is the first step toward preventing them.

UNDERSTAND THE DANGER

What Causes Wildfires?

Wildfires need an ignition source, such as lightning or human activity. Dry, windy conditions can help a fire spread.

Natural Causes

Lightning can ignite wildfires. Hot, dry weather dries vegetation and can help an existing fire spread more quickly.

Human Activities

Unattended campfires, discarded cigarettes, and burning rubbish can start dangerous fires.

Dry and Windy Conditions

Dry vegetation, low humidity, and strong winds can help fires spread quickly across large areas.

BE PART OF THE SOLUTION

Small Actions Can Save Forests.

Never leave a campfire unattended. Avoid burning rubbish near forests. Follow local fire warnings and report wildfires to the appropriate authorities.
